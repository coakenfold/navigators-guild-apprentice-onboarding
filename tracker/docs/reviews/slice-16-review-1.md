## Verdict

Slice 16 does what PLAN.md asks. `digest` lists each project's auto-verified items since its last ack, judge-passed first, then every shelve, `approve`, `reject`, `criterion accept`, `delete` and trigger-switching `edit`, oldest first, each marked by terminal. `digest ack` asks for `ack` through `tty::confirm`, builds the digest again under the lock, compares whole digests, and records nothing with exit 1 if anything changed. Every history time it compares against is taken under the lock, after the re-read, and `digest ack` holds the lock until the clock passes its own second ([main.rs:4139-4161](../../src/main.rs#L4139-L4161)), so "strictly after the ack" can't miss an entry written in the same second. `review` flags an overdue digest, `audit` reports every sign PLAN.md lists plus mismatched holds, and `judges` splits its counts by the held question's trigger and answer, as slice 15's review 2 asked. `cargo fmt --check`, `cargo clippy -- -D warnings` and `cargo clippy --all-targets -- -D warnings` pass, and all **559 tests pass**, the count and split the readme gives. TR-12 was tracked as the rules ask: `a877beb` was committed at 21:00:35Z, 69 seconds before `start`, all eleven checks failed then, every commit up to the approve is scoped to TR-12, and the user approved its T1, T7 and T8 holds from a terminal, `human` at `93637e2`.

On a copy of the real store, the first `tracker digest` lists TR's 18 auto-verified items and 14 actions (13 approves and TR-1's `criterion accept`) in 218 lines, never acknowledged. `digest ack --project TR` under `script` printed all of it again, 222 lines on stderr, cancelled on `TR`, and acknowledged on `ack`. `audit` found no problems in 54 items, in about a second. `judges` is empty, since no TR item has a judge. `review` says "Nothing needs you.", and will until 2026-11-28, seven days after the oldest entry.

What matters most:

- **Bug 1:** `digest ack` builds the digest without any item file it can't read, and doesn't say so, so an auto-verified item that's unreadable at that moment is acknowledged unseen and never listed again. Reproduced, and unlikely.
- **Security 1:** `digest ack` run under `script` acknowledges like a human, and the same `script` recipe is what the digest exists to catch. §7 names moving the ack time by editing the store, but not this.
- **Design 1:** §3 says `review` shows "whether a digest is pending", and the code shows only an overdue one, so the user's first, 32-entry digest won't show in `review` for four days.
- **Design 2:** a judge-passed item's digest entry shows refs, answers and reasons, but not the question that was asked, though §2.6 lists it and §6 relies on the digest to catch vague questions.

TR-39, TR-44, TR-47, TR-48 and TR-50 are still open, as the readme says.

## Bugs

1. **`digest ack` acknowledges a digest built without the item files it can't read, and doesn't say any were left out.** `project_digest` drops `load_items`' skipped files ([main.rs:4100-4105](../../src/main.rs#L4100-L4105)), and `digest_ack` puts only the digest's text in the warning ([main.rs:4112-4137](../../src/main.rs#L4112-L4137)), so neither the question nor the result mentions them. `digest` prints the skipped note, but the ack doesn't. Reproduced: XX-1 and XX-2 auto-verified, XX-2's file replaced with `{ broken`, `digest ack --project XX` under `script` showed only XX-1, accepted `ack` and printed "1 item auto-verified and 0 actions", with no word of a skipped file. With XX-2's file put back, `digest --project XX` said the digest was empty, though `list` shows XX-2 done: its `verification.at` is before the ack, so no digest will list it. §2.7 says the ack exists so that "nothing I haven't seen is marked as seen", and §4.4 says a gate fails closed on such a file, since it may be the item that matters. Unlikely: it needs an item file that can't be read at the moment of the ack. But the harm is the one the digest is there to prevent, an auto-verified item nobody read. Fix: **(a)** refuse `digest ack` with exit 3 while a file in `items/` whose name puts it in the project can't be read, naming it, as G1 does at `start`. **(b)** Put the skipped note in the warning, and leave the choice to me. I'd pick (a), with a test in the shape of the reproduction.

2. **A judged shelve tells the reader to look for a reopen and `start` that no digest lists.** For a shelve of an item with a judge criterion, `action_text` says "starting it again empties its T11 count, so look for a reopen and start after this" ([main.rs:4072-4077](../../src/main.rs#L4072-L4077)). But `build` lists only shelves, `approve`, `reject`, `criterion accept`, deletes and two kinds of `edit` ([digest.rs:162-172](../../src/digest.rs#L162-L172)): a reopen and a `start` are never in the digest, before or after the ack. Found by reading. Low: TR has no judge criterion yet, and the hint is right that the pair is worth a look, only wrong about where it is. Fix: **(a)** say "look in `tracker show <ID>` for a reopen and start after this". **(b)** List a reopen of an item with a judge criterion. I'd pick (a): one line, and §2.6 doesn't ask for reopens.

3. **`audit` calls a ref it couldn't check "a sign that the store was edited by hand".** When the project root can't be read, or git fails, `ref_problem` returns "refs/tracker/<ID> can't be checked: …" as a `ref` problem ([audit.rs:130-133](../../src/audit.rs#L130-L133), [audit.rs:146](../../src/audit.rs#L146)), and `audit` heads every problem with "each a sign that the store was edited by hand" ([main.rs:4196-4199](../../src/main.rs#L4196-L4199)). Reproduced: with the throwaway repo renamed, `audit` reported "3 problems … each a sign that the store was edited by hand", two of them "can't be checked: XX's root, …, can't be read". A moved repository isn't a hand edit. Likely whenever a project's repo moves, and low harm: the line itself says what happened. Fix: **(a)** word the heading "each a sign that the store was edited by hand, or something audit couldn't check". **(b)** Give "can't be checked" its own `problem` kind in [audit.schema.json](../../schemas/audit.schema.json), listed under its own heading. I'd pick (a).

## Security

1. **`digest ack` run under `script` acknowledges like a human, and replaces the only record of the ack before it.** The ack's terminal check is `tty::confirm`'s, which §7 says `script` gets around, and the ack records `"interactive": true` ([main.rs:4146-4152](../../src/main.rs#L4146-L4152)). `digests.json` keeps one entry per project, so the previous ack is overwritten. Reproduced on the copy of the real store: `digest ack --project TR` under `script`, typing `ack`, recorded `{"at": "2026-11-23T23:30:54Z", "interactive": true, "verified": 18, "actions": 14}`, and the next `digest` was empty. The digest is how I'd spot an `approve` or `delete` made under `script` (§2.6, [DESIGN.md:816](../DESIGN.md#L816)). An agent that uses the same recipe on `digest ack` empties it, and what's left is the digest's "last acknowledged at …" line, which I'd have to remember wasn't mine. §7 names an agent "moving the digest's ack time forward" by editing the store, but not this, which needs no store edit, and whose recipe is in [tests/digest.rs](../../tests/digest.rs). Unlikely, since it's a deliberate move, and high harm, since it hides everything else the digest shows. Fix: **(a)** say it in §7's bullet on the terminal check, and have `review` and the digest name the last ack's time and counts, so an ack I didn't make stands out. **(b)** Keep every ack in `digests.json` rather than the last, and list the acks since my last look at the top of the digest. I'd pick (a), since §7 already accepts that the terminal check is a convention.

## Where the code and DESIGN.md disagree, or the design is unclear

1. **§3 says `review` shows "whether a digest is pending", and the code shows only an overdue one.** §3's `review` row ([DESIGN.md:451](../DESIGN.md#L451)) says the inbox shows "whether a digest is pending". `review` prints only digests that `is_overdue` passes, and says "Nothing needs you." otherwise ([main.rs:3740-3744](../../src/main.rs#L3740-L3744), [main.rs:3847-3865](../../src/main.rs#L3847-L3865)). §2.6 says only that an overdue digest shows ([DESIGN.md:354](../DESIGN.md#L354)), so the two sections disagree. Reproduced on the copy of the real store: with TR's 32-entry digest never acknowledged, `review` said "Nothing needs you.". The oldest entry is TR-1's `criterion accept` at 2026-11-21T05:28:22Z, so it turns overdue on 2026-11-28. Certain, and low harm: the digest waits, which is all it does. Fix: **(a)** make §3 say "whether a digest is overdue (§2.6)". **(b)** Have `review` add one line for a digest with entries that isn't overdue yet, such as "TR's digest has 32 entries, due on 2026-11-28", keeping "Nothing needs you." for an empty inbox. I'd pick (a), since §2.6's weekly rhythm is the point, and a line after every auto-verify would make the inbox never empty.

2. **A judge-passed item's entry shows refs, answers and reasons, but not the question that was asked, or the item's commits.** §2.6 says the digest lists "the commits, and each judge's questions with their refs, answers and reasons" ([DESIGN.md:354](../DESIGN.md#L354)). `verified_text` prints the record's one commit and, for each judge, `judge c1:works answered yes: "…"` ([main.rs:4008-4015](../../src/main.rs#L4008-L4015)). `Verified` carries only the criteria's texts ([digest.rs:43-50](../../src/digest.rs#L43-L50)), so a judge criterion shows its `text` and not its questions. Found by reading. The readme's demo output shows the same: "The code does the work", then "judge c1:works answered yes", and never "Does this code do the work?". §6 counts on the digest to catch vague judge questions ([DESIGN.md:789](../DESIGN.md#L789)), and that needs the question's words. Certain for every judge-passed item, and none exists in TR yet. Fix: **(a)** print each answer's question beside its ref, taken from the item's criterion (`c<n>`'s judge, the question whose `id` ends the ref), and make §2.6 say "the commit it was verified at" rather than "the commits". **(b)** Make §2.6 say the digest shows refs and that `tracker show` has the questions. I'd pick (a).

3. **`unchecked` done items are in no digest, and §2.6 doesn't say so.** `build` lists an item only when its record is `auto` ([digest.rs:147-150](../../src/digest.rs#L147-L150)). In the real store, 18 TR items are done `unchecked` (TR-1 to TR-4 and TR-13 to TR-26, from the binaries before slice 8), and none will ever be listed, though no human looked at them either, which is how the Terms table defines auto-verified. Found on the copy of the real store. Low: only binaries before slice 8 write `unchecked`, PLAN.md's integration risks asked for those records to be read by hand, and each was in a slice review. Fix: **(a)** list `unchecked` items in the digest under their own heading. **(b)** Say in §2.6 that `unchecked` items aren't listed, since they predate the gates. I'd pick (b).

- **Decide design 1 and security 1's wording,** since both are about what the user sees first: whether `review` should mention a pending digest, and whether §7 names `digest ack` under `script`.

A merge commit or a fast-forward keeps them. `refs/tracker/<ID>` keeps each verified commit either way, so `audit` stays clean.
- **PLAN.md's status rows** become `merged` in the pull request that merges each branch, not before, as PLAN.md says. The open items TR-39, TR-44, TR-47, TR-48 and TR-50 don't block a merge, but TR-47 should be done before any project adds a `diff` judge.
